**Cleaning Up the Commentary for Deeper Analysis**

Okay, so I'm looking at these Shogi game commentaries, and the goal is clear: I need to whip them into shape for serious analysis.  They're filled with all sorts of noise – the meta-data, the introductions, the timing stuff – all important for the human experience, but frankly, just cluttering up the signal I'm after.  I want the core, the essence of the game as expressed through the board positions and the critical moves. I need to get rid of the fluff and get down to brass tacks.

The process is straightforward enough: I have a set of rules, and I'll use those to filter out the irrelevant parts.  It's about identifying the commentary elements that contribute to our understanding of the game's evolution from a computational point of view, and discarding everything else. Essentially, I'm parsing the human language to extract the game's actual "language," if you will, the language of the pieces on the board.
