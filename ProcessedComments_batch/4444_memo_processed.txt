**Structuring and Cleaning Up Shogi Game Commentary**

Okay, I've got a task here that's right up my alley. Someone wants me to take raw shogi commentary, the kind you get after a match, and *clean it up*. They're looking for something that just focuses on the critical stuff: the state of the board and the specific moves made. No extraneous fluff, just pure analysis.

So, here's how I see this working:

1.  **Iterate through each move.** That's the baseline. I'll read the commentary one move at a time.
2.  **Identify the chaff.** There's a clear list of things to remove: anything that falls under that umbrella of "unnecessary information." I'll need to define that meticulously, but that's my starting point.
3.  **Delete the noise.** Once the unwanted segments are flagged, it's straight to the editing. Remove the irrelevant comments.
4.  **Polish the prose.** What remains has to flow, has to make sense. I'll need to rephrase things if necessary to make it read smoothly and naturally. It's about preserving the *analysis*, not just the raw data.
