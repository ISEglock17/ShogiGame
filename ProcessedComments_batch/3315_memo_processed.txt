**Focusing the Lens: Cleaning Up Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I've got a task here. Someone, probably a fellow aficionado, wants me to surgically remove the extraneous noise from shogi commentary. They're aiming for a lean, mean, analysis-focused narrative of the game. My job is to transform raw commentary into a streamlined sequence of moves and their accompanying explanations, devoid of anything that wouldn't directly help a keen observer dissect the strategy.

The request boils down to cleaning up the commentary by removing specific types of information. It's a bit like filtering a chess game. I need to be a digital surgeon, excising anything that isn't directly related to the current board state and the rationale behind each move.

Here's the hit list of elements to eliminate:

*   **Personality Profiles:** Any mention of players' names, ranks, or their affiliations – it's all noise here.
*   **Historical Detours:** References to prior games or precedents? Gone. We're only focused on the board in front of us.
*   **Temporal Ticking:** Time spent on each move is useful for real-time play, but it will be removed.
*   **Player Bios:** Details about their professional history, ranking, career achievements, and where they trained. All irrelevant.
*   **Style Snippets:** Any discussion of players' distinct techniques will be cut.
*   **Venue Vibes:** Information about the physical environment, location, weather conditions, or lodging will be ignored.

I'm essentially creating a distilled version of the game. Nothing more, nothing less. Just the bare essentials to understand the ebb and flow of strategic thinking.
