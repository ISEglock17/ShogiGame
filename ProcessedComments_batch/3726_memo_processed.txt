**My Approach to Refining Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I'm presented with Shogi game commentary and my goal is clear: I need to take this often verbose text and distill it down to its core. I'm focusing on the *essence* of the game, the actual board positions and the reasoning behind each move.  Think of it as removing the noise and highlighting the signal, so to speak.

The user wants me to follow specific rules for this process. Basically, I have to *get rid* of all the fluff. I'm talking about metadata that's not directly related to the game's flow. Introductions, player bios, even timestamps on the moves... all of that is clutter in this context.

What *I'm* aiming for is a streamlined, coherent narrative. A narrative solely dedicated to explaining the board states and the rationale of the players' decisions. The user wants the explanations to read naturally, as if someone is actually explaining the moves to you. The key is to convey the "why" and "how" of each move, the strategic context. I want the user to be able to understand the sequence of events of the game purely from the comments, without being distracted by extraneous information.
