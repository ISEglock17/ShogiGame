**Focusing the Lens: Cleaning Up Game Commentary for Analysis**

Okay, so the user wants me to clean up these Shogi game commentaries, right? My task is pretty straightforward: get rid of all the fluff and leave behind the juicy stuff – the board positions and the actual moves. Think of it like a meticulous edit, stripping away the unnecessary noise so we can really dive into the core of the game.

I've got a set of rules to follow, a sort of checklist to guide me. Anything that doesn't directly contribute to *understanding* the game is going out the window. That means:

*   **No Names, No Titles:** Bye-bye to everyone's names, ranks, and who their master is. We're interested in the *game*, not the personalities.
*   **History is History:** Forget past games or precedents. We're only focused on the present move and its immediate context.
*   **Time's Up:** No need to dwell on how long each move took. That's irrelevant to the strategic analysis.
*   **Player Bios? Delete:** No mention of the player's personal details, win/loss stats, titles, or numbers. I care about the play, not the player.

I'm thinking of this as a sort of "data sanitization" process. I'm taking the raw commentary and transforming it into something clean, concise, and valuable for deeper analysis. The goal is to provide a pure stream of game-relevant information, free from any distracting clutter.
