Okay, so I'm presented with a task. Someone wants me to clean up these comments that come with Shogi game data. The goal is clear: they're cluttered with extraneous details that mess up proper analysis. I need to keep the juicy stuff - the commentary explaining the board state and the moves being played - and discard the rest.

Think of it like this: I need to separate the signal from the noise. I'm going to carefully read each comment, and then, according to this specific set of rules, I'll filter out the irrelevant bits. I need to get rid of all that metadata, the introductions, the player biographies, the time stamps - everything that isn't directly related to understanding what's happening on the board.

My process will be straightforward: I'll go comment by comment, make the determination based on the rules, and then decide what to keep and what to cut. It's all about making sure that the final output is a clean, coherent stream of commentary that's actually *useful* for deeper analysis of the games.
