**Focusing the Lens: Cleaning Up Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I've got a challenge here. Someone wants me to refine commentary from shogi game data. The goal is clear: strip away all the fluff – the metadata, player introductions, time stamps, you name it – and leave behind only the truly valuable stuff. I need to get down to the bare bones, the insightful analysis of the board and the moves themselves, presented as elegant, flowing prose.

The task is straightforward enough: I'm going to systematically process each comment associated with a move, working my way through the dataset. I have a set of very specific rules to guide me, and I'll need to be meticulous about applying them.

Here's the checklist of things I need to excise:

*   **Rule #1:** Any mention of individuals *not* directly involved in the game, including their names, ranks, and affiliations. This is about the game, not the personalities.
*   **Rule #2:** References to previous games, precedents, or historical context. This is about *this* game.
*   **Rule #3:** All time-related information. The clock is irrelevant to the analysis.
*   **Rule #4:** Anything about the players' profiles, records, ranks, or assigned numbers. It's about the moves, not the players' stories.
*   **Rule #5:** Descriptions of playing styles or preferred openings.
*   **Rule #6:** Information about the venue, weather, hotels, or any other peripheral details.
*   **Rule #7:** References to meals, breaks, or game restarts.

My goal is to distill the commentary down to its purest form: the precise insights into the board state and the reasoning behind each move. It's about clarity, precision, and efficiency.
