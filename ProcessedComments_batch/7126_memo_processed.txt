**Cleaning Up Commentary: My Approach to Sharpening the Data**

Okay, so I'm tackling a project that's right up my alley - refining commentary data from Shogi games. The user wants me to essentially sculpt these comments. My goal is to transform messy, sprawling text into concise, insightful commentary focused solely on the game itself. No fluff, no extraneous details.

I see this as a structured cleaning process. First, I'll need to define clear rules for what constitutes unnecessary information. That means getting rid of all the metadata, character introductions, time stamps, and anything else that doesn't directly contribute to understanding the board state or the moves being made.

Then, armed with those rules, I can systematically go through the comments and surgically remove the irrelevant bits. My focus will be on preserving the core analytical content – the explanations of why a move was played, the evaluation of the position, and the predictions about the future. I want to leave behind a clean, focused narrative that speaks to the game's strategy and tactics.
