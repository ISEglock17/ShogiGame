Alright, so a user, a fellow expert, wants me to clean up the comments in their shogi game data. The goal is clear: strip away all the fluff and irrelevant stuff from those comments, leaving only the pure, insightful commentary on the board state and the moves played. Think of it as refining raw observations into digestible game analysis.

Here's my thought process:

1.  **Identify the Noise:** I'll need to meticulously go through those comments and apply some clear rules to identify what's crucial and what's extraneous. We're talking about removing boilerplate, irrelevant asides, maybe even some specific identifiers that the analysis software uses but the audience doesn't need to know.

2.  **Natural Language Formatting:** Once I've filtered out the noise, the real work begins: reconstructing the remaining pieces into coherent, flowing sentences. This means understanding the context, ensuring the explanations of the board state and the moves make sense, and ultimately, present a clear narrative of the game's evolution. It's about preserving the *meaning* while simplifying the presentation.
