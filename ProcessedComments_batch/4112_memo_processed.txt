**Streamlining Shogi Commentary: My Approach**

Okay, so I'm tasked with cleaning up these Shogi commentary snippets. The goal is clear: I need to transform these raw comments into concise, focused explanations of the game itself.  My objective is to remove all the fluff – the metadata, player introductions, time stamps, you name it – and leave behind only the essential, insightful commentary on the board's state and the moves being made.

Essentially, I'm aiming to distill the essence of the commentary into a series of flowing, logical sentences that would be perfect for someone doing a deep analysis of the game.

I've got a set of rules to follow, a checklist of sorts. Here's how I'm thinking I'll work through each comment:

*   **First, I'll filter out the noise related to non-players.** If it's not directly related to the current move or board position, it goes.
*   **Next, I'll purge the history.** Anything referencing past games or similar situations is irrelevant for my purpose.
*   **Time is of the essence, but not literally.** Any time-related details will be zapped.
*   **Player profiles? Gone.** Their background and accolades, while interesting, are not crucial to understanding the move itself.
*   **Talking about play style and general strategies?** I'll have to consider those based on each rule.
*   **And finally, all information related to the location, weather, or venue will be tossed away.**

It's all about precision and clarity, right? I'll analyze each comment with these rules in mind, carefully extracting and rephrasing the core information about the board and the moves.
