They're looking for a refined version, something that cuts straight to the chase – the essential stuff for serious analysis. I get it. We're not interested in the fluff; we want the meat and potatoes of the play itself.

My goal is to distill these comments down to their core meaning: the board state and the reasoning behind each move.  I need to strip away all the distracting elements. Anything that doesn't directly contribute to understanding the position or the strategic thinking – the introductions, the player biographies, the clock readings – all that's gotta go. I need to leave behind only the natural, insightful commentary about *what* happened on the board and *why*. It's about preserving the essence of the game, not the packaging.
