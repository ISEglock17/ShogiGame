**Cleaning Up My Commentary for Analysis**

Okay, so I'm dealing with a lot of Shogi commentary data, which is great because it means there's a wealth of information available. But, as we all know, raw data is rarely useful in its current form. My goal here is to refine this commentary, focusing specifically on the core elements: the explanations of the board state and the reasoning behind each move. This means getting rid of all the extraneous noise – the metadata, the introductions of the players, time information, and anything else that's not directly related to the actual analysis.

My process will be straightforward. I'll need to define clear rules about what constitutes "unnecessary information" and then apply those rules to scrub the comments. The end product should be clean, concise explanations that highlight the tactical and strategic insights of each move, ready for deeper analysis. It's about extracting the signal from the noise and making the data truly valuable for my research.
