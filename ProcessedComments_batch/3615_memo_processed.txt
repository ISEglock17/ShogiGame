**Structuring Shogi Commentary for Clarity**

Okay, so someone's asking me to clean up and structure shogi commentary. Essentially, they want me to take raw text from a game and make it more readable and focused.  The goal is to provide a clear, concise analysis, devoid of the distracting noise that often creeps into live commentary.

Here's the approach I'm taking: I need to build a system to identify and remove irrelevant information. This means filtering out things that don't directly contribute to the analysis of the board state and the moves played.

Specifically, I need to eliminate:

*   **Mentions of people not directly involved in the current game:** No extraneous names, ranks, or information about their affiliations. We're focused on the *game* itself, not the periphery.
*   **Historical context that doesn't advance the current analysis:** Any references to past games, precedents, or irrelevant tactical considerations should be scrubbed out. The focus should be on the *present* move and its implications.
*   **Temporal noise:** Time-related data like thinking time or specific clock readings are distracting to the flow of analysis and should be removed.

Once I've identified and removed this extraneous information, I'll rewrite the remaining text into a more natural and fluid form. The goal is to provide a focused, insightful, and easy-to-follow summary of the game's key moments and the players' thought processes.
