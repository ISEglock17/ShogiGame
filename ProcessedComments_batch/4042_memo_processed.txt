**Focusing the Analysis: Streamlining Game Commentaries**

Okay, so I'm presented with a task, and it's a familiar one: a user, likely a fellow expert in the world of Shogi, wants to clean up the commentary data they've got. The goal is clear, and it's something I've grappled with many times in my own analysis.  We need to distill the essence of the game.

What they've got, I imagine, is a mountain of information—the raw material of a brilliant match. But it's cluttered with the usual suspects: the opening formalities, player introductions that quickly become irrelevant, and the ever-present time stamps that tell a story of the clock, but not necessarily of the strategy. It's all extraneous noise when we're trying to extract strategic insights.

So, here's the real challenge: to strip away the fluff. To be left with the core – the descriptions of the board state, the explanations of moves, the *why* behind each decision. To create a narrative that flows naturally, a commentary that illuminates the thinking behind the moves, not the process of recording them. That's the key to making the data useful for deeper analysis and strategic learning.
