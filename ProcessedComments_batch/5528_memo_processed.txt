**My Approach to Refining Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I'm being asked to clean up some Shogi game commentary. The goal is to strip away all the fluff that's irrelevant for analytical purposes, leaving behind only the concise explanations of the board state and the moves themselves. It's about distilling the essence.

Essentially, I'll be working through the comments for each move based on a predefined set of rules. First, I have to *identify* and *eliminate* all the information that doesn't contribute to understanding the position or the rationale behind the moves. That could be anything from unnecessary remarks about the players' demeanor to redundant descriptions of standard opening sequences.

Then, and this is crucial, I need to take what's left and re-craft it into clear, naturally flowing sentences. I'll need to make the descriptions sound like part of a coherent explanation of the game, rather than a jumbled collection of notes. It's a bit like taking a rough draft and polishing it into something ready for publication.
