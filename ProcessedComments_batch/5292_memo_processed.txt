**My Approach to Refining Shogi Game Commentaries**

Okay, so I've got this Shogi data set, specifically the commentaries accompanying each move. My task is pretty clear: I need to clean these up, transforming them from their raw, sometimes noisy state into concise, digestible analyses. Think of it like a really targeted editing job.

My goal is to distill the essence of each move's meaning. That means stripping away all the fluff – the metadata about the game, the player introductions, and even the time spent on a move. Basically, anything that doesn't directly contribute to understanding the board state or the reasoning behind the move itself has to go. I want to keep only the pure, insightful commentary on the position and the specific tactical or strategic thinking behind the play.

And I have a specific set of rules to follow – a well-defined process to guide this transformation. It's a structured approach, which I appreciate. First, I identify the information to be excised based on these rules. Once I've filtered out the irrelevant parts, then I will focus on restructuring the core commentary into natural-sounding sentences.
