**My Approach to Refining Shogi Game Commentary**

Okay, so I'm presented with a task, right? Someone wants me to take this raw data from shogi games - you know, the comments that come with the moves - and whip them into something clean and useful. Essentially, they want me to refine the commentary.

My goal here is pretty straightforward. I need to strip out all the noise. Get rid of the stuff that's just cluttering things up for analysis. That means the metadata, the introductions of the players, the time stamps... all the irrelevant fluff. What I want to *keep* is the heart of the matter: the actual explanations of the board positions and the reasoning behind each move. I'm after the core insights.

My process will be methodical, I think. I'll need a set of solid rules to guide me, and I'll use those to make decisions about what to delete and what to keep. It's like curating an exhibit, I suppose, but for shogi commentary. My end product should be a collection of well-formed, readable sentences that focus solely on the game's strategic nuances.
