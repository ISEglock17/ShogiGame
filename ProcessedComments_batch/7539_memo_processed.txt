**Focusing the Commentary: Streamlining Shogi Analysis**

Okay, so I'm presented with a task, and it's a familiar one: a shogi game's commentary needs a serious clean-up.  Essentially, I'm being asked to transform the raw text accompanying each move – the kind you get with game records – into something much more useful for analysis.  Think of it like this: I need to strip away all the extraneous noise.  The biographies of the players? Gone. Time stamps? Useless clutter. Any general introductions or editorializing? Adios.

The goal is laser-focused: I want the *essence* of the commentary. Just the clear, natural-language explanation of what's happening on the board, and why. The insights into each move, the strategic reasoning, the tactical considerations – that's the gold I'm after.  I need to distill it down to the core explanation of the board state and the player's intentions.  This streamlined commentary is what will ultimately feed my deeper understanding and allow for more effective analysis.  This is about extracting the true value from these commentaries.
