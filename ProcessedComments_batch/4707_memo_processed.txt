**Focusing the Lens: Cleaning Up Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I've got a challenge here. Someone wants me to refine a bunch of commentary from shogi games. Essentially, they want me to strip away all the extraneous noise and leave behind the pure, distilled analysis of the board and the moves. Think of it like a chess grandmaster meticulously annotating a game - they're only interested in the *chess*.

My job is to go through these comments, identifying and removing anything that doesn't directly contribute to understanding the game's evolution. That means purging:

*   Any mention of people *not* actively playing, their ranks, affiliations, or mentors. These introductions are distracting from the flow of the game analysis.
*   References to historical precedents or past encounters. We're interested in *this* game, in this moment.
*   Time stamps and time-related details. The timing of a move doesn't inherently contribute to the analysis of the position on the board.
*   Biographical information about the players, their records, ranks, and affiliations. Again, it's about the *moves*, not the *players' careers*.

I need to apply these rules consistently. The goal is to produce clean, concise, and insightful commentary, ready for deeper analysis. It's about distilling the game to its essential elements.
