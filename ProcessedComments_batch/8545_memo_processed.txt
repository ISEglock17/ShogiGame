**My Approach to Refining Shogi Commentary**

The goal is clear: strip away everything that isn't directly related to the actual moves and board state, and leave behind a concise, natural-language explanation of each hand. I see the process as a two-step transformation.

First, the heavy lifting: I need to analyze each comment and employ a set of rules to identify and ruthlessly eliminate the fluff. That's the meta-information, the player introductions, the time stamps—anything that, while perhaps interesting contextually, isn't essential for the analytical understanding of the game itself.

Second, with the core commentary in hand, the real work begins: reconstructing those fragments into flowing, coherent sentences. I need to make sure the descriptions read smoothly and logically, giving a clear picture of what's happening on the board with each move. This requires a certain finesse to convey the strategic nuances within the limited context, but that's what I'm here for.
