**My Approach to Refining Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I'm tasked with cleaning up these shogi commentary sections from game data. The goal is clear: I need to transform the raw text into concise, insightful explanations of the moves and board states. It's all about making the commentary readable and useful for analysis, stripping away the clutter.

My thought process is pretty straightforward. First, I'll go through the comments and identify the noise. That means anything that's not directly about the game itself – the metadata, player introductions, time stamps, irrelevant background information, and so on. Anything that doesn't contribute to understanding *why* a move was made or *what* it means for the board will get the axe.

Then comes the fun part: I'll take the remaining information and craft it into a coherent narrative. The goal isn't just to *remove* information, but to *reframe* it. I'll focus on the strategic implications of each move, the potential threats and opportunities, and the overall flow of the game, creating a clear and logical progression of analysis for the user.
