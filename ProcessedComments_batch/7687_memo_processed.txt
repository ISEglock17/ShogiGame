**Refining Commentary for Deeper Game Analysis**

Okay, so I'm presented with a task, a familiar one: someone wants to clean up commentary from Shogi game data. They've got this trove of game logs, rich with annotations, but it's all a bit… cluttered. My job is to transform this raw data into something truly useful for analysis.

What they need is focused. I have to strip away the fluff: the metadata about the game itself, the introductions to players, the time stamps... all the things that aren't *directly* about the board state and the moves being played. It's about getting down to the bare bones of the strategic discussion.

The key is in the details, of course. I need to use the given rules to identify those elements that should go. This will be the bulk of the work. Then, and only then, can I focus on the final goal - weaving the remaining insights into coherent, natural sentences. This way, the commentary becomes an asset, not an obstacle to understanding the game's subtleties.
