**Streamlining Commentary in Shogi Data**

Okay, so I understand the task. Someone wants me to clean up the comments within a Shogi game dataset. Essentially, they want me to refine the text, removing irrelevant bits and leaving only the essential explanations of the board state and the moves.

Here's how I'm thinking I'll approach this:

1.  **Iterate through the Data:** I'll read through each comment associated with each move in the game. That's the foundation of the operation.
2.  **Rule-Based Filtering:** I'll need to define clearly what constitutes "unnecessary information." I'll need a set of rules – a deletion criteria – to identify the specific segments of text that need to go. This could be anything from redundant phrases to irrelevant commentary that strays from explaining the board or the moves.
3.  **The Chop:** Once I've pinpointed the parts that violate the rules, I'll excise them, essentially "trimming the fat" from each comment.
4.  **Polish the Prose:** After the removal, I'll need to make sure the remaining text flows naturally in Japanese. The goal is a concise and informative comment that actually makes sense when read standalone.
5.  **Standardization of Notation:** Finally, to maintain consistency, I'll replace the actual names of the players with "Sente" (先手) and "Gote" (後手) wherever they appear. That'll keep the focus on the moves and the analysis, not on the specific players in any given dataset.
