**Focusing the Lens: Cleaning Up Game Commentary for Deeper Analysis**

Okay, so the user wants me to clean up these Shogi game commentary datasets. I understand the goal: they want to strip away all the fluff – the metadata, player introductions, time stamps, all that noise – and leave behind only the pure, distilled essence: the explanations of the board positions and the rationale behind each move. It's like taking a complex piece of music and isolating just the melody, removing the orchestration.

My approach is straightforward, really. First, I need to define the rules, the criteria for what's expendable and what's core. Then, I'll filter the commentary, applying those rules to delete the irrelevant stuff. Once that's done, I'll finesse what remains, polishing it into a smooth, natural-sounding narrative. This should make it much easier for them to analyze the games without getting bogged down in unnecessary details. It's all about focus, really. And in this case, focus means extracting the essential wisdom hidden within the game's commentary.
