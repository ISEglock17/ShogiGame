**Focusing the Lens: Cleaning Up Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I'm presented with a pretty common task in analyzing shogi game data: cleaning up the commentary.  Essentially, I need to sift through a bunch of notes attached to a game record and strip out all the extraneous stuff. My goal is to leave behind only the pure, distilled analysis of the board state and the moves being played. Think of it like a chemical process – removing the impurities to get to the core substance.

My brain's already firing up the filtering logic.  I've got a mental checklist of what needs to go:

*   **No Names, No Bios:**  I'm ruthlessly cutting out any mention of people *besides* the players themselves. That means no grandmaster introductions, no "disciple of [famous Sensei]", none of that. It's about the game, not the players' resumes.
*   **History is Irrelevant:**  References to prior games, precedents, or what happened in the past are noise. We're interested in the *current* state and the reasoning behind each move *as it unfolds*.
*   **Time Flies, But We Don't Care:**  Clock readings, move times – gone. Those are irrelevant to the pure strategic analysis I'm after.
*   **Keeping it Lean:** Profiles, match records, ranks, and player numbers... all of those are extraneous detail. The commentary needs to stand on its own as a logical explanation of the game's flow. It's about preserving the game's intrinsic narrative.

Essentially, I'm transforming the commentary from a human-readable stream of consciousness into a concise, focused analysis. It's all about making the data work for *me* – so I can quickly and efficiently extract the key insights from these games.
