**Cleaning Up My Commentary for Analysis**

Okay, so I'm presented with a task, and it's something I've dealt with often in my own research: refining commentary data from shogi games. Essentially, I need to take a raw collection of commentary, the kind that accompanies game records, and strip away all the extraneous noise. The goal is clear: to get down to the pure essence of the analysis, the insightful explanations of board positions and move choices, presented in a clean and natural way.

I've got a system in mind, a structured approach to this. First, I'll meticulously go through each comment, applying a set of rules – my own rules, honed through experience. These rules will guide me in identifying and removing irrelevant information. I'll be looking for things like meta-commentary, introductory remarks about the players, any time-based data... anything that doesn't contribute directly to the strategic understanding of the game. My focus will be razor-sharp: what information actually helps illuminate the decision-making process? Once that unnecessary material is removed, what's left will hopefully be a refined, focused, and truly useful set of insights.
