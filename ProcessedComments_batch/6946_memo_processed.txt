**Focusing the Signal, Drowning Out the Noise**

Okay, so I'm presented with a pretty common task in my line of work: cleaning up commentary from shogi games. My objective is crystal clear: take these raw comments, which are inevitably cluttered with a bunch of irrelevant data, and refine them into concise, pure analyses of the board state and the moves being made.

Essentially, I need to strip away all the fluff. I'm talking about getting rid of all the metadata, the introductions of the players, any time-related information – anything that doesn't directly contribute to understanding the intricacies of the game itself.  What I'm aiming for is a stream of natural-sounding explanations, solely focused on the positions of the pieces and the reasoning behind each move.  It's about extracting the essence of the analysis and presenting it in its most digestible form.  It's a process of distillation, really.
