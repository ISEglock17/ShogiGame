I need to strip away all the extraneous stuff - the metadata, the personal introductions of the players, those pesky time stamps - and leave only the pure, distilled essence of the board analysis and the explanation of each move. That's the real gold, the meat of the insights, the part I need to truly understand the game.

The process itself is straightforward, at least in theory. Step one: I need to scrutinize each comment, applying my expert eye to identify the parts that fall under the "delete" category based on the defined rules. Then, I can focus on extracting the good stuff.
