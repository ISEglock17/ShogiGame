**Focusing the Lens: Cleaning Up Game Commentary**

Okay, so I'm tasked with a familiar problem in my line of work: refining game commentary from shogi matches. The core of my goal here is to get down to the brass tacks – the actual, insightful analysis of the board and moves.  I need to strip away all the extraneous noise that clutters the data. Think of it like a sculptor chipping away the marble to reveal the statue within.

Essentially, I'm going to follow a two-step process. First, I'll need to develop a set of clear rules to identify and remove all the stuff that's not directly related to explaining the gameplay. I'm talking about introductions, character descriptions, time stamps – anything that's metadata and doesn't actually contribute to a deeper understanding of the board. Second, with the "unnecessary" stuff gone, I'll focus on polishing what remains into clear, cohesive sentences that really illuminate the tactical and strategic reasoning behind each move. It's about clarity and conciseness, so that the raw analytical gold can be easily mined.
