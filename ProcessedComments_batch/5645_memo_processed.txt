**Focusing the Signal: Cleaning Up Shogi Commentary**

Right now, I'm thinking about how I can refine these Shogi game comments. My goal is to transform the existing text, which is likely a mix of useful insights and extraneous details, into a streamlined, high-value dataset for analysis. It's about extracting the *essence* of the game commentary.

I need to filter out the noise – any information that doesn't directly contribute to understanding the board state or the reasoning behind specific moves. This means ditching the metadata, the personal introductions, and the time stamps. What I'm after are the clear, concise explanations of the moves themselves, delivered in a way that feels natural and flows smoothly. I'm essentially performing a data "decluttering" exercise, only retaining the information that truly matters for deep strategic understanding. I can then use this cleaned data to build much more effective models for things like move prediction or game-state evaluation.
