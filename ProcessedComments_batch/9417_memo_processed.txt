**Focusing the Lens: Cleaning Up Game Commentary for Analysis**

Okay, so the user wants me to clean up these Shogi game commentaries. The goal is clear: strip away all the fluff – the metadata, the introductions, the time stamps – and leave behind only the pure, insightful commentary on the board and the moves themselves. Think of it like a distilled essence of the game's strategic flow.

My process will be methodical. I'll take each comment and apply the given rules, systematically eliminating any information that doesn't directly contribute to understanding the game's unfolding dynamics. It's like removing noise from a signal, isolating the valuable data.

The rules I'm working with are well-defined and straightforward:

*   **Rule 1:** Anyone who isn't one of the players involved in *this* specific match? Gone. Names, ranks, affiliations, all irrelevant.
*   **Rule 2:** References to past games, precedents, historical data? Out of here. We're focused on the *now*, the current board state.
*   **Rule 3:** Timestamps, durations, any temporal information? Not needed. The focus is on the *moves*, not how long they took.
*   **Rule 4:** Player profiles, records, ranks, and any information about the player's history? All to be deleted. We're interested in the *game*, not the players.

This is all about efficiency and precision. By applying these rules, I aim to create a clean, concise, and focused set of commentaries that are ideal for deep analysis.
