**Cleaning Up Commentary for Deeper Analysis**

Okay, so I'm looking at this dataset of Shogi game records, and specifically the commentary that goes along with each move.  The raw stuff is a bit of a mess, though.  There's all this extra information, like metadata, player bios, even timestamps, mixed in with the actual analysis of the position and the reasoning behind each move.  Frankly, it's making it harder to get at the good stuff.

My goal is to distill the commentary down to its essence. I want to keep only the crucial bits: the descriptions of the board state and the explanations of why a particular move was played.  Think of it like getting rid of the fluff in a research paper - you want the core argument and supporting evidence, nothing more.  This means stripping out all the unnecessary noise, the biographical details and time references, and keeping only the natural language explanations of the strategic and tactical considerations at play. I'm after the pure, distilled insights that will allow for deeper analysis of the game itself.
