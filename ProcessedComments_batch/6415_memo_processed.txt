**Focusing the Lens: Cleaning Up Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I've got a challenge here. Someone wants me to refine a bunch of commentary from Shogi games. The goal is clear: strip away all the fluff and irrelevant details and leave behind the pure, distilled essence of the board and the moves. Think of it like a chess master analyzing a game – they don't care about the pre-game introductions or how long the players took to decide a move, they're focused on the *analysis* itself.

My job is to go through each comment, one by one, and meticulously filter out all the noise. I'm talking about things like player introductions, biographical details, mentions of past encounters, or any timing information. Anything that doesn't contribute directly to understanding the current board state or the reasoning behind a specific move needs to go.

Essentially, I'm aiming to create a streamlined, readable narrative that focuses *solely* on the strategic thought process. No distractions, just pure Shogi analysis. It's about preserving the critical insight, not the window dressing.
