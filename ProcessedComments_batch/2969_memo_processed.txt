**My Approach to Refining Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I'm tasked with cleaning up these Shogi game commentary datasets. The goal here is pretty clear: I need to strip away all the extraneous fluff – the metadata, player introductions, time stamps, you know, the stuff that's not directly relevant to the actual analysis of the game itself.  I want to focus on the truly valuable bits: the positions on the board and the explanations of the moves. Essentially, I'm building a more streamlined, more digestible version of the commentary, something that feels like natural prose.

My plan is straightforward: I'll go through the comments, apply the established rules to identify and eliminate the unwanted information.  Then, with what's left, I'll work on making sure everything flows smoothly, transforming the remaining bits and pieces into coherent sentences that highlight the key points of the game's evolution. It's about getting to the core of the analysis, and leaving the rest behind.
