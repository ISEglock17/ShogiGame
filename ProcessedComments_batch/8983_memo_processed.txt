**Cleaning Up the Commentary for Deeper Analysis**

Okay, so I'm presented with a task, a familiar one: refining commentary data from shogi games. The core of it is quite straightforward. I need to take these often-verbose comment sections and strip away all the extraneous noise. We're talking about removing the fluff – the metadata that isn't directly related to the actual moves and board states. Things like player introductions, time stamps for each move (that's already implicit in the record), and general observations that don't contribute to understanding the positional analysis.

My goal is to distill the commentary down to its essence: the *meaningful* explanations of the board positions and the reasoning behind each move. I want to retain the natural flow of the explanations, but eliminate anything that's purely contextual or tangential to the strategic evaluation of the game. It's about getting to the meat of the analysis, the tactical and strategic insights, without the distractions.
