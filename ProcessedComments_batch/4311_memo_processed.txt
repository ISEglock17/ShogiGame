**My Approach to Refining Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I'm presented with a shogi game commentary, and the goal is to clean it up. The user, being the expert they are, wants me to extract the *essential* information, focusing on the board state and the moves themselves, all in a natural flow.  Essentially, I need to make it more readable for someone who really *gets* shogi.

Here's my thought process on how I'll tackle this:

1.  **Iterative Processing:**  I'll go through each move's commentary sequentially, one after the other.
2.  **Rule-Based Filtering:** For *each* sentence within the commentary of *every* move, I'll analyze it against a pre-defined set of rules. I'll need a good grasp of the exclusion criteria so I don't mistakenly remove something crucial.
3.  **Identification & Logging:**  When I find a sentence that violates a rule, I'll flag it for deletion. Critically, I'll note *why* it's being deleted (which rule it breaks). This is important for debugging and ensuring I'm being consistent.
4.  **Natural Language Polishing:**  The remaining sentences will need to be rephrased if required to make them sound natural and grammatically correct. It's about providing fluent insights, not just a list of factual statements.
5.  **Content Verification:** If after removing the irrelevant sentences, there's absolutely nothing left that describes the game's progress (the move or the state of the board) in any way, then the entire commentary for that move will be discarded.  It's just unnecessary clutter in that situation.
6.  **Formatted Output:** Finally, the cleaned-up commentary, alongside the list of reasons why parts were deleted, must be rendered in the specific format the user has requested. Keeping in mind, they want this for analysis or publication; It needs to be precise and useful.
