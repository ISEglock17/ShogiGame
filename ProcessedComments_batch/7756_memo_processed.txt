**Focusing the Lens: Cleaning Up Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I've got a challenge here. Someone wants me to refine a shogi commentary dataset, specifically the comments attached to individual moves in game records. The goal is clear: they want to strip away the noise and get down to the essence of the game analysis. I need to sift through the commentary and pull out only the gems - the clear explanations of the board state and the reasoning behind each move. Think of it like a textual shogi puzzle: I need to identify the key pieces of information and discard the irrelevant distractions.

Essentially, I'm going to have to make a structured approach to the text, using the defined rules for the content.
* **Removing Meta-Information**: Get rid of anything that doesn't directly contribute to understanding the game's flow. That means deleting any mentions of people *not* involved in the current match. Player profiles, rankings, and affiliations? Gone.
* **Eliminating Time-Sensitive Data**: Dump the clocks and time stamps. The focus is on the *moves*, not the speed.
* **Removing references to previous records**: That way, the record is fully self-contained.

The core principle is focus. I need to make the process more streamlined, in the same way, the user would like the record to be.
