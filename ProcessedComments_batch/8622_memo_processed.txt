**Cleaning Up Game Annotations**

Okay, so I need to refine a set of Shogi game annotations, the kind you find embedded in game data. The goal is clear: strip away all the extraneous noise and leave behind only the pure, insightful commentary on the board state and the moves themselves. Think of it like distilling the essence of the analysis.

This means getting rid of everything that isn't directly related to the strategic thought process. That includes all the metadata – who the players are, background on their history, anything about the time control or the match conditions. I'll also be removing any mentions of time elapsed or who's thinking. Essentially, anything that's *about* the game but not *of* the game.

The end product should be a coherent narrative that flows naturally, explaining the rationale behind each move and the resulting shifts in the game's dynamic. It's about preserving the analytical core, the heart of the commentary, in a way that remains valuable for deeper analysis or study.
