**My Approach to Refining Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I'm presented with Shogi game commentary and I need to clean it up. The goal here is pretty clear: I've got to strip away all the fluff. I'm talking about the meta stuff, any introductions of the players, time stamps – essentially anything that doesn't directly contribute to understanding the board state or the reasoning behind the moves.  I want to leave only the juicy bits: the actual explanations of what's happening on the board, the strategies being employed, and the analysis of each move.  It's about crafting a narrative that focuses solely on the game itself, not the periphery.  It's a process of extracting the core chess insight from the surrounding noise, leaving behind a clear and insightful analysis.
