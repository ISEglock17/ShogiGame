**Focusing the Commentary: Streamlining Shogi Analysis**

Okay, so I've got a challenge here. Someone wants me to refine commentary found within Shogi game records. The goal is clear: strip away all the extraneous noise – the metadata, player introductions, time stamps, and anything irrelevant to the game itself – and leave behind only the pure, insightful commentary on the board's state and the moves made.

My task is to process these comments, one move at a time, adhering to a set of rules. I need to be laser-focused on the relevant information.  Essentially, I'm surgically removing the fluff.

Specifically, I'm looking for the following:

*   **Rule #1:  No Personal Biographies:**  Anything about people other than the current players - their names, ranks, who their teachers were – needs to go. That's context, not commentary.
*   **Rule #2:  Forget the History:**  References to prior games, precedent setting moves, or related historical information are a distraction and I'm removing it.
*   **Rule #3:  Time is Irrelevant:**  All time-related details – how long someone thought, the game's start and end times – are just noise, and should be eliminated.
*   **Rule #4:  No Player Profiles:** Removing descriptions of the players' past performances, ranks, player numbers, or who trained them is an important step.

I understand the importance of clear, focused commentary for effective game analysis.  This is about getting to the heart of the moves themselves.  Let's get to work.
