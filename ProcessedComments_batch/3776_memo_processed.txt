**My Approach to Refining Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I'm presented with this Shogi game data, and the user wants me to clean up the commentary. It's all jumbled with metadata, introductions, timestamps – all the stuff that's not actually the *chess* analysis.  My goal is clear: I need to extract the core, the essence of the game's explanation, and present it in a flowing, natural way.

Here's how I see this process:

First, I'll need to define some hard and fast rules.  I'll look at the data and identify the "noise" – the stuff that's not relevant to understanding the moves. This includes things like the game's setup, player introductions, the exact time each move was made, and any extraneous remarks. I'll flag these elements for removal based on a pre-defined set of criteria.

Next, I'll focus on the actual chess analysis.  I'll scan for descriptions of the board state after a move, reasoning behind a move, and tactical or strategic considerations. I'll need to recognize these elements within the text, even if they are embedded amongst other information. My aim is to be left with the distilled game analysis, without any fluff.

Then comes the final step. I need to take the remaining information and form it into sentences which make sense and read fluently. After all, the cleaned-up comments are meant to support understanding of the analysis and the game. I may need to re-order the comments to provide a coherent flow. It's a bit like taking a complex equation and writing it out in plain English.
