**My Approach to Refining Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I'm presented with this shogi commentary data, and the goal is clear: to clean it up and make it useful for analysis. It's essentially a filtering and refining process. I need to take these often lengthy, conversational comments and distill them down to the core meaning – the actual tactical or strategic explanations of the moves.

My mental process is pretty straightforward. First, I'll *carefully* read through each comment. I'll be looking for specific elements to *eliminate*: the fluff, the biographical details about the players, the timestamps, anything that doesn't contribute directly to understanding the position or the rationale behind a move. I know these elements, according to my defined rules, are essentially noise.

Then, the *real* challenge begins. Once I've stripped away the extraneous bits, I need to reshape what's left into something readable. It's about taking fragments and weaving them into coherent, naturally flowing sentences. The goal isn't just to *retain* information, but to *present* it in a way that makes sense to someone deeply familiar with shogi.

Finally, I need to wrap it all up. My cleaned-up comment needs to be outputted in a specific format, and I need to keep track of *why* I removed certain sections. This means documenting the filtering process, for each and every chunk of the original commentary that I have altered. This kind of documentation allows for verification of the process.

This is my core task: to convert this raw, unstructured commentary into a clean, concise, and valuable resource for deep shogi analysis.
