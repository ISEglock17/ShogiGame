**Structuring the Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I'm presented with a bunch of Shogi game commentary and my task is to clean it up, make it more streamlined, more focused on the *moves* themselves, the *strategy*, the *current board state*.  I need to be ruthless, almost surgical, about what I keep and what I discard.

Essentially, I'm going to go line by line through this commentary, following very specific guidelines for removal. Anything that doesn't directly contribute to the *flow* of the game description is out.

Here's the checklist of things that are *definitely* getting the boot:

1.  Anyone *not* directly involved in the current match - their names, ranks, affiliations, any of that. The focus is on the players *on the board*.
2.  Any mention of precedent, past games, historical context. The current moment is all that matters.
3.  Time information. We don't need to know how long they're taking, or the specific time on the clock.
4.  Detailed bios of the players - their records, ranks, player numbers, who their mentor was.  That's background noise.
5.  Descriptions of their play style or preferred openings.  Let the moves speak for themselves, at least for now.
6.  The setting - where they're playing, weather, accommodations - all irrelevant. 
7.  Anything else extraneous, of course.

So, it's about distillation.  The *essence* of the game, move by move, stripped of the excess. Time to get to work!
