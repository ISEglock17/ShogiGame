**Focusing the Commentary: Streamlining Shogi Analysis**

Okay, so I'm presented with a collection of comments from a Shogi game record. The goal is clear: clean up these comments. I need to strip away all the extraneous noise and leave behind only the pure, insightful commentary on the board's state and the moves made. This is all about getting to the core of the game's evolution, not the peripheral details.

My approach is systematic. I've got a set of rules to follow, a checklist to ensure I'm only keeping what truly matters for analysis. First, anything related to people *outside* of the two players is gone: names, ranks, affiliations, all gone. No need for distractions there. Second, references to past games or precedents? Out! I want to focus on *this* game, *this* moment. Third, time stamps? Irrelevant. The clock doesn't dictate the flow of analysis. Fourth, profiles, records, styles, and affiliations of the players themselves -- that doesn't belong in the core commentary. I'm after the substance of the game, not the backstory. And finally, I'm tossing anything location-specific (hotel information, weather, the room itself). It's all about the moves on the board. With these rules in place, I can start honing in on the truly valuable parts of the text.
