**My Approach to Refining Shogi Commentary**

Okay, so I'm tasked with cleaning up these Shogi game commentary datasets. The goal here is pretty straightforward: get rid of all the fluff and extraneous details that aren't crucial for analyzing the game itself. That means I need to excise all the metadata, player introductions, time stamps – anything that doesn't directly contribute to understanding the board state or the reasoning behind the moves.

My plan is simple but effective. First, I'll go through the comments and apply a set of rules to identify and remove all the unnecessary information. I've got a good grasp on what constitutes relevant game analysis and what doesn't, so this part shouldn't be too tricky.

Then, the really interesting part begins: I'll take what's left and reshape it into coherent, readable sentences. The goal isn't just to strip away the irrelevant; it's to elevate the quality of the remaining commentary, making it easier to understand and to derive meaningful insights from the moves.
